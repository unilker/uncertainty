# 1. Start here

Welcome. This guide explains measurement uncertainty from the very beginning, in plain words, and shows you how to do every kind of evaluation with this program. You do not need any statistics to start. If you already work with uncertainty budgets, you can jump straight to the chapters that interest you.

> **Tip:** Every chapter can be opened from any screen: press the book icon on the home screen, or the **?** icon in a project, which opens the chapter about that kind of project.

## How the program is organised {#program}

**The home screen** has two parts:

- **Start a new evaluation**: one card for each kind of evaluation. A card opens a new project filled with a small example, so you never start from an empty page. Chapter [8](08-choosing-a-project-type) helps you choose the right card.
- **Example library**: 286 worked examples from guides, standards and papers. Each one has been recomputed and checked. You can filter them by level: *Beginner*, *Intermediate* and *Advanced*.

**A project** opens in a window with tabs:

| Tab | What you do there |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Title, description, and for library examples *What this example teaches* and *Step by step, in plain words* |
| **Model** or **Data** | The heart of the project: the measurement model and its inputs, or the data of a laboratory method |
| **Settings** | Coverage factor, Monte Carlo trials, rounding of the result |
| **JSON** | The project file as text, for experts |
| **Results** | Everything the program calculated, after you press **Calculate** |

The red **Calculate** button runs the evaluation. **Save** stores the project as a `.json` file, and the menu (⋮) exports the report as PDF, HTML or Markdown, in English or Turkish.

## Learn with the examples {#examples}

Every example in the library is also a lesson:

1. Open an example from the library (start with the *Beginner* level).
2. Read **What this example teaches** and **Step by step** on the **Overview** tab.
3. Press **Calculate** and open **Results**. Compare the numbers with what the explanation says.
4. Change something (an uncertainty, a reading, the coverage factor) and calculate again. Nothing can break: the library copy stays unchanged, and your changes are only saved if you press **Save**.

Chapter [13](13-learning-with-examples) lists all examples by level, with what each group teaches.

> **Try:** Open [Mass of a powder by weighing](example:nist-tn1900-e1-weighing), the simplest example of the library, and follow the four steps above.

## Words and symbols used in this guide {#symbols}

- **y** is the result you want, **x₁, x₂, …** are the input quantities.
- **u** is a standard uncertainty, **u_c** the combined standard uncertainty, **U** the expanded uncertainty, **k** the coverage factor.
- Numbers are written as in the program: a point as decimal separator in English, a comma in Turkish.
- Words in **bold** are buttons, tabs or fields of the program; words in *italics* are new terms explained nearby.

Chapter [3](03-key-words) explains every term in plain words, with a small example for each.
